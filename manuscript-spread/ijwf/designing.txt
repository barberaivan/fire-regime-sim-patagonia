        Why these four:
          - overlap runs 0.84 / 0.64 / 0.35 / 0.27, a clean monotone gradient
            from the best fit in the set to nearly the worst (the 57-fire median
            is 0.535, the worst is 1999_27j_N at 0.149);
          - sizes run 20 ha to 28,567 ha, i.e. the smallest class up to the
            largest fire in the whole record;
          - bias covers both directions and both magnitudes: unbiased (1.04),
            mild overestimate (1.11), strong UNDERestimate (0.44, the strongest
            in the set) and strong OVERestimate (2.67, the strongest);
          - none of them is a split fire, so no panel needs a caption caveat.

        The panels tell a story in that order: the model can nail a fire
        (1999_25j), it holds up on the largest one (2015_50), and it fails in
        both directions on mid and small fires (2004_23, 2002_34).

        Swaps, if a different balance is wanted:
          - want a LARGE underestimate: 2021_2146405150_E (8,342 ha, ov 0.499,
            q 0.74) or 1999_2140469994_r (26,976 ha, ov 0.390, q 0.75)
          - want a LARGE overestimate with bad overlap: 2021_865 (9,662 ha,
            ov 0.240, q 1.61) or 2015_16 (982 ha, ov 0.340, q 1.89)
          - want a small fire the model gets RIGHT: CoCampana (18.5 ha,
            ov 0.750, q 1.02)
          - the true worst overlap is 1999_27j_N (518 ha, ov 0.149) but its q is
            1.02 with a 0.00-3.80 interval, so it reads as noise, not as bias.
